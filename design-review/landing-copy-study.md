Nothing on the page has changed yet. This is for review.

## What the page says now

| Where | Copy |

| Scene 2 | **Ask.** |
| Scene 3 | **Everyone, connected.** |
| Close | **Start with one afternoon.** · button: Get started |

Two problems:

- **The hero is the category's stock line.** Remento, the closest competitor, has a section heading that reads "THEIR STORIES, IN THEIR WORDS". Ours is almost word for word the same.
- **No sentence says what Firepit is or does.** A visitor has to work it out from the animation, and there is no sub-headline at all.

## Patterns worth taking

1. **The headline names the thing, or the moment, plainly.** Granola, Day One and Linear each say what the product is in under eight words. The mood comes after.
2. **One sentence under it explains how it works.** Remento's "They just talk. Remento captures it, writes it…" and Granola's "Notes, actions and memory. Without a meeting bot." Both are short and concrete, and both say what *you* don't have to do.
3. **The difference is said against the alternative.** Granola: "Humans in the room, not bots". For Firepit the alternatives are a voice memo nobody replays and a book nobody finishes writing.
4. **Section headings are short verb phrases about the user's moment,** not feature names. "Give your full attention" works where "Ask." and "Everyone, connected." read as labels.
5. **The button says the first step,** sometimes adding that it's free, rather than "Get started".
6. **Proof sits under the hero** (numbers, logos, quotes). Firepit has none yet, and PRODUCT.md rules out invented claims, so this section stays out until it's real.

What *not* to take:

- The category's sentimental language ("forever", "a gift of a lifetime", "whole new light").
- PRODUCT.md's banned promise claims, which rule out "forever" and anything about privacy or storage.

## Three rewrites

Each keeps the page's existing scenes; only the words change.

### 1 · Plain: name it, then say how it works (Granola / Day One)

- **Hero:** Record the family stories before they're gone.
  - *alt:* Interview your parents. Keep what they say.
- **Under it:** Sit down and talk. Firepit listens, then turns the conversation into stories in their own words, dated and linked to the people in them.
- **Button:** Start your first interview
- **Scene 2 (Ask):** Ask what they said. If they never said it, Firepit tells you so.
- **Scene 3 (People):** See who knew whom.
- **Close:** One conversation is enough to start. · button: Start your first interview

### 2 · The moment: lead with the afternoon itself (Remento's how-it-works, more cinematic)

- **Hero:** You ask. They talk. The story stays.
- **Under it:** Firepit records a conversation with your parent or grandparent and writes it up as stories, quoting their exact words.
- **Button:** Start an interview
- **Scene 2:** Come back with questions. Every answer points to where they said it.
- **Scene 3:** Every name they mention, connected.
- **Close:** Start with one afternoon. · button: Start an interview *(the current line works here, at the end)*

### 3 · Against the alternative (Granola's "not bots")

- **Hero:** Not a voice memo you'll never replay.
- **Under it:** Firepit turns a conversation with your relative into stories you can read, search and share: their words, with the dates and people in them.
- **Button:** Record a conversation
- **Scene 2:** Search their stories like you'd ask them.
- **Scene 3:** The people, the places, the years, all linked.
- **Close:** Your first conversation takes an afternoon. · button: Record a conversation

**Recommendation:** start from 1 or 2. Option 1 is the clearest for a first-time visitor. Option 2 fits "direct, a little cinematic" best. Option 3 leans on a negative, which is strong as a section heading but weak as the first thing someone reads. One line can be mixed in from another option, e.g. option 2's hero with option 1's line under it.
